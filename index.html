Microfluidic Device Design and Tumor-on-a-Chip Modeling Sept 2025 – Dec 2025

UCR. Designed and fabricated a two-inlet, single-outlet microfluidic chip and characterized its laminar flow behavior and diffusion gradient formation using colored-liquid flow visualization, demonstrating controlled concentration mixing across the channel.

  • Developed a COMSOL Multiphysics model of a tumor-on-a-chip device with two-inlet asymmetric diffusion-dominated species transport in brain tumor microenvironments.
  • Analyzed velocity magnitude and concentration profiles across the chip geometry to evaluate drug accessibility in poorly-perfused tumor regions, identifying diffusion as the dominant transport mechanism limiting drug delivery under low-flow conditions.

Gene-to-Protein Expression and QC Analysis Sept 2024 – Dec 2024

UCR. Transformed pET28b-YPet-Ubc9 plasmid into E. coli BL21(DE3) via electroporation; validated the transformation by restriction enzyme digest and PCR, confirming a ~1.2 kb insert against a ~5.0 kb vector on gel electrophoresis.

  • Performed Sanger sequencing and bioinformatics sequence alignment to classify constructs as wild-type, contaminated, or mutant, identifying point mutations at base pairs 991 and 1044.
  • Expressed recombinant His-tagged YPet-Ubc9 via IPTG induction and purified it using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography following probe sonication cell lysis.
  • Quantified protein concentration via Bradford assay and assessed purity by SDS-PAGE, confirming the expected ~44 kDa band.
  • Validated protein-protein interaction between CyPet-SUMO1 and YPet-Ubc9 using FRET spectroscopy across 12 concentration pairs, identifying optimal binding signal at 1 μM CyPet / 100 μM YPet.

Parametric Fluid Flow Characterization Sept 2024 – Mar 2025

UCR. Developed parametric COMSOL Multiphysics models to characterize airflow and hepatic blood flow under controlled variable conditions, comparing healthy and disease states.

  • Modeled airflow velocity through a healthy versus a mucosal-lined respiratory system, showing how mucosal accumulation narrows airways and impairs oxygen delivery.
  • Compared surface velocity in healthy versus fibrotic liver lobules, showing that scar tissue reduces flow uniformity and increases stagnation.
  • Applied experimental design methodology to isolate and quantify the impact of individual variables on system performance.
